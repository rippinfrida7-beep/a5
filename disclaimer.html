Safety Advisory

Astronomical & Solar Safety Disclaimer

Effective Date: Autumn 2026 • Scope: Solar Observation & Desert Safety

The celestial journals, solar sundial mathematics, astrolabe guides, and dark sky exploration protocols published on CosmicDialDune are provided exclusively for informational, historical, and educational purposes. Solar observation, optical astronomy, and desert field expeditions involve inherent physical hazards that demand absolute caution and situational awareness.

1. Critical Direct Solar Observation Eye Warning

NEVER look directly at the Sun with the naked eye, binoculars, cameras, or telescopes without ISO 12312-2 certified solar filters. Concentrated solar rays passing through unfiltered optical lenses cause irreversible retinal burns and permanent blindness within milliseconds. When operating sundials, astrolabes, or gnomons, always observe the projected shadow of the sun on the dial plate—never sight directly through pinholes or tubes toward the solar disk.

2. Desert Wilderness & Extreme Thermal Safety

Arid desert environments present severe environmental extremes, including daytime temperatures exceeding $110^\circ\text{F}$ ($43^\circ\text{C}$), rapid nocturnal temperature drops near freezing, venomous fauna (rattlesnakes, scorpions), and total absence of cellular communications. Observers embarking on night sky stargazing expeditions must carry abundant drinking water (minimum one gallon per person per day), thermal layers, satellite SOS beacons, and physical topographic maps.

3. Optical Instrument & Laser Pointer Etiquette

Green astronomical laser pointers used for constellation identification must never be pointed toward low-flying aircraft, roadways, or human observers. When attending dark sky observatories or star parties, use only red-filtered flashlights to preserve dark-adapted scotopic night vision and avoid blinding fellow observers.

4. Archaeoastronomical Site Protection & Heritage Laws

Ancient desert solar observatories, petroglyphs, and solstice alignments are sacred cultural heritage sites protected under federal and international antiquities statutes (e.g., Archaeological Resources Protection Act). Never touch petroglyphs, move alignment stones, or erect temporary structures on prehistoric monuments.

5. No Clinical or Navigational Warranty

While our solar calculators and spherical trigonometric formulas are derived from standard astronomical algorithms, they must not be relied upon as the sole means of life-critical navigation in remote wilderness. Users assume full personal responsibility for their navigation, equipment readiness, and safety in desert terrain.
